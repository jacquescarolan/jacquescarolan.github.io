I am an applied physicist and neuroscientist developing next-generation neurotechnologies to help understand and ultimately repair the human brain. I think a lot about how we can get frontier treatments to the people who need them most. I am currently a founding Programme Director at the [Advanced Research and Invention Agency](https://aria.org.uk/) and an Honorary Associate Professor at University College London (UCL).

I spent the first decade of my career working in applied physics, where I was developing photonic technologies to accelerate quantum and classical computing, initially at [MIT](https://www.rle.mit.edu/quantum-photonics-group/) and later at the [Niels Bohr Institute](https://nbi.ku.dk/english/research/quantum-optics-and-photonics/). I then pivoted into circuit neuroscience at [UCL](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/medical-sciences/divisions/wolfson-institute-biomedical-research/research/neural-computation), where I developed optical techniques using optogenetics and high-speed two-photon voltage imaging to study the awake, behaving brain.

{{< /text-image >}}

I completed my PhD at the [University of Bristol](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/research/groups/pho/). I have been awarded a [BBSRC Discovery Fellowship](https://www.ukri.org/opportunity/early-independence-bbsrc-fellowships-outline/), a [Marie Skłodowska-Curie Global Fellowship](https://marie-sklodowska-curie-actions.ec.europa.eu/actions/postdoctoral-fellowships) and attended the [66th Lindau Nobel Laureates Meeting](https://www.lindau-nobel.org) on behalf of the Royal Society.




### Experience

- **2023–present — Founding Programme Director,** [ARIA](https://aria.org.uk/). I lead the Precision Neurotechnologies and Massively Scalable Neurotechnologies programmes, working with around 30 teams across a £120M research portfolio.
- **2021–2024 — Senior Research Fellow,** [UCL](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/medical-sciences/divisions/wolfson-institute-biomedical-research/research/neural-computation). I worked with Michael Häusser in the Neural Computation Laboratory on circuit neuroscience, combining optogenetics with high-speed two-photon voltage imaging to study synaptic plasticity in the awake brain.
